# Business viability

Source: D-CONTENT-AND-PITCH Phase 7. Four concrete revenue paths. **Users never
pay, and there are never ads** — an attention-economy revenue model would
contradict the entire product thesis.

### 1 · Sponsor & CSR conservation pools (directed dāna)
Nepali banks and corporates already fund heritage under CSR obligations. The app
turns diffuse goodwill into itemised, sponsor-funded conservation needs
(`seed/needs.json`): "Puskarini drainage clearance, NPR 180,000." Merit directs
where a sponsor's money goes; the money moves sponsor → custodian directly. **We
never handle funds** — which is also what keeps us clear of financial regulation.

### 2 · Tourism-board & authority licensing
Nepal Tourism Board and the Lumbini Development Trust have a direct interest in a
maintained, multilingual visitor layer over the site. License the platform per
property. The same engine re-skins for the Kathmandu Valley monument zones.

### 3 · Export tooling as a service to heritage authorities
The real deliverable is the conservation time series and its CIDOC-CRM export.
Sell the monitoring + export tooling to the Department of Archaeology and to
custodians who must file state-of-conservation reports — a workflow they already
owe UNESCO, done better and cheaper.

### 4 · Partner redemption (non-cash)
Merit redeems for museum entry, a monastery guesthouse meal, or local craft —
tiers arranged with partners, never a currency conversion. This drives footfall
to local businesses and monasteries without merit ever becoming money.

---

**The one-line answer:** "Sponsor and CSR conservation pools, tourism-board
licensing, and the export tooling as a service to heritage authorities. Not
users. Never ads."
