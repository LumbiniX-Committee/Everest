The Technology Stack, and What It Takes to Scale It

A conservation-evidence platform distributed through pilgrimage — from one heritage site to a national register.

1 The load Scope: the platform that carries the evidence. Model training, survey method and content editorial are covered elsewhere.

What the stack has to carry

Sākṣī produces one irreplaceable artefact: a photograph taken from a known point on a known day, with enough metadata that a photograph from the same point years later can be compared against it. Every choice below answers to that artefact, and to four properties it must hold.

It must survive the absence of a network. A phone in the Sacred Garden can go hours without usable signal, and the moment of capture cannot be rescheduled. The device is the source of truth; the server is a copy. Anything that assumes connectivity at write time is disqualified whatever its other merits.

It must be durable on a decadal horizon. A series is worthless if its early frames are lost or unreadable — which rules out proprietary media formats, lossy re-encoding of originals, hard deletes, and any storage whose exit cost is unbounded.

It must stay honest under load. A sensor-verified alignment lock and a shot framed by eye must stay distinguishable at a million records exactly as at a hundred, which makes provenance a schema property rather than a UI convention.

It must be affordable to an institution, not a venture round. The buyer is a site trust or a department of archaeology, and a licence that scales with visitor numbers is not a business model here — it is why a deployment never happens.

The architecture in one sentence

An append-only ledger on every device, a replicated Postgres of record in the cloud, immutable content-addressed media in object storage, and inference pushed to whichever side of that boundary can be held accountable for its answer.

The stack that scales, layer by layer

Scaling here does not mean more requests per second. It means more sites, more custodial institutions, more languages and a longer record, without weakening the guarantee that a record made offline in 2026 is legible and trustworthy in 2046.

3.1  Client — React Native · Expo SDK 57 Stay on React Native and Expo keep

The camera, GPS, magnetometer, accelerometer and two AI runtimes all sit behind native modules, and Expo's config plugins keep that native surface declarative instead of a hand-maintained android/ folder. The New Architecture is already on, and the vision module requires it.

Rejected: Flutter, which discards ~55,000 lines and both AI integrations for a rendering model this product does not benefit from; and native twin apps, which double the field-testing cost of the one loop that matters.

3.2  Local ledger — SQLite, append-only SQLite as an append-only evidence ledger keep

Observations, reports and corrections are inserts. A mistake is fixed by a superseding row that names what it supersedes, never by an UPDATE over history. That makes the record auditable, and it also makes synchronisation tractable: an append-only log has a far smaller conflict surface than mutable state. Unknown measurements are stored as NULL, never zero — a zeroed GPS accuracy is indistinguishable from a perfect fix, and one such row poisons a decade of series analysis.

3.3  Sync — the one real replacement PowerSync between SQLite and Postgres swap

The hand-written queue is correct for one site and one device per person, and fails on the three things multi-site operation introduces at once: partial replication (a guide in Patan must not carry Lumbini's whole corpus), multi-device authorship, and conflict when a custodian acts from both phone and dashboard.

PowerSync streams Postgres logical replication into on-device SQLite bidirectionally, with declarative sync rules scoping exactly which rows a device receives, behind a first-class React Native SDK. The sync-rule model is the feature being bought; bidirectionality is table stakes.

Rejected: ElectricSQL, whose read-path sync is elegant and whose write path is the weaker half — the wrong trade for a product whose entire value is the write. Turso solves a latency problem this application does not have.

3.4  Identity — provenance without an account wall Anonymous sessions, upgradeable in place add

Demanding a login before a visitor can contribute kills the funnel the product depends on. Demanding nothing costs three things: the record cannot name its author, personal history cannot survive a reinstall, and row-level security can only be scoped to a role rather than a person. An anonymous session issued at first launch and later linkable to an email resolves all three without a wall in front of the capture.

3.5  Cloud of record — Postgres, PostGIS, pgvector One Postgres, three extensions keep

PostGIS turns site boundaries, vantage geometry and "what within 500 m has gone longest without a resurvey" into an index rather than an application loop, and makes the GeoJSON export a real GIS artefact rather than a courtesy. pgvector holds the Dhamma corpus embeddings: published guidance puts an HNSW index at sub-10 ms up to roughly 50 million vectors, and this corpus sits four to five orders of magnitude below that ceiling, so a dedicated vector database would add an operational component, a second consistency story, and no measurable latency.

Row-level security scoped to author and custodial institution is the authorisation model. Keeping policy in the database rather than an API tier lets phone, dashboard and any future integration share one rule set.

3 continued Media and tiles are where a heritage platform quietly goes bankrupt. Both are fixed by owning the bytes rather than renting access to them.

3.6  Media — the real cost centre re-seat

Photographs are the product, and the only part whose volume grows without bound. Ten thousand observations a year at four megabytes each is forty gigabytes of originals — trivial to store, expensive to serve carelessly.

  • Originals are immutable and content-addressed. The object key is the SHA-256 the capture-integrity migration already computes: re-upload is idempotent, corruption is detectable, and no process can silently rewrite an original.
  • Derivatives are generated, never authoritative. Thumbnails, then-and-now plates and dashboard previews are reproducible from the original, and may be rebuilt at will.
  • Object storage with zero egress fees — Cloudflare R2, or any S3-compatible store on the same terms. A then-and-now plate travelling round a heritage newsletter is a success, not a bandwidth invoice.
  • A cold tier for originals past two years. Consulted rarely; never deleted.

3.7  Maps — own the basemap re-seat

MapLibre stays as the renderer; the tile supply moves to a PMTiles archive — one Hilbert-ordered pyramid file served from object storage over HTTP range requests, with no tile server to run. Two things follow:

  • Offline map packs become a file copy. A regional extract downloaded before a visit is exactly the artefact the format is built around — the difference between a map that works in the Sacred Garden and one that does not.
  • Per-view pricing disappears. Published figures for self-hosted PMTiles on zero-egress storage run to cents per month in storage and well under a dollar per million range reads, against commercial tile APIs charging low single-digit dollars per thousand map loads. At pilgrimage volumes that is the difference between a rounding error and a line item that scales with the thing we most want to grow.

4 Intelligence Two models, two entirely different accountability regimes. Neither is allowed the last word.

The AI stack

4.1  Vision, on the device

A segmentation model proposes crack candidates on the photograph just taken, drawn as dashed boxes so nothing reads as a verdict; a person confirms severity, and the report records that the AI assisted. It runs on the phone because the network is unavailable at the moment it is needed, and because a photograph of a monument should not have to leave the country to be looked at.

ONNX Runtime with the XNNPACK provider stays the portable baseline. ExecuTorch is the recommended second path where latency justifies it: 1.0 general availability in October 2025, a roughly 50 KB runtime, and vendor backends for Qualcomm, MediaTek, Arm and Apple — the only realistic route to NPU execution on the mid-range Android hardware this product actually meets in the field. The two coexist behind one interface, and the device picks.

4.2  Language, grounded or silent

Dhamma retrieves passages from a fixed corpus — the Pali canon alongside the ICOMOS Venice and Burra Charters, UNESCO World Heritage records, and named Kathmandu Valley archaeology — writes an answer using only those passages, and verifies every claim resolves to a retrieved span. When it cannot, it refuses. Source text and citations are never machine-translated.

Scaling this is not a bigger model. It is a bigger evaluation: the answer-quality gate should grow into span-level faithfulness scoring of the kind the RAGTruth corpus and the public hallucination leaderboards established, run in continuous integration, so a corpus addition or model change that raises unsupported spans fails the build rather than shipping quietly. Model access belongs behind a provider-agnostic gateway: the corpus, the retrieval, the verifier and the refusal policy are the asset; the generating model is a replaceable part.

The exit test

Every managed service here is chosen so that leaving it is a migration rather than a rewrite. Postgres is Postgres, S3-compatible storage is a byte copy, SQLite and PMTiles are files, and MapLibre and ONNX Runtime are open source. The one place this could quietly break is the sync layer, which is why the on-device schema stays legible on its own terms: were PowerSync removed tomorrow, the ledger on every phone and the Postgres behind it both remain readable.

6 Not buying A stack is defined as much by what it refuses as by what it adopts.

What we deliberately do not adopt

A dedicated vector database

The corpus sits four to five orders of magnitude below the scale at which pgvector stops being the right answer. Adding one buys a second consistency model and no latency.

A microservice backend

One aggregate — the observation — and one hard constraint, the offline write. Splitting that across services multiplies the failure modes of the only path that must never fail.

Kubernetes

A few thousand writes a day against managed Postgres. A cluster adds an on-call rotation a heritage trust cannot staff.

A gamification layer

Streaks and points reward volume, and volume is the wrong incentive for evidence. Merit here is puṇya: unscored, unspendable, untransferable — and a linter fails the build if the vocabulary creeps back.

Client-side AI verdicts

The detector proposes; a person disposes. Letting a model set severity makes the dataset unusable as evidence, which is the only thing it is for.

Analytics in the capture path

Location traces of individuals at religious sites are not telemetry. Measurement is aggregate and first-party, or it does not happen.

In summary

The honest finding is that three seats need replacing and the rest needs defending. Sync moves to a replication engine that understands partial datasets; media and tiles move onto storage the project owns outright, so success in distribution is not punished by price. Everything else — React Native and Expo, SQLite as an append-only ledger, one Postgres with PostGIS and pgvector, on-device vision, and retrieval that refuses rather than guesses — holds, because it was chosen against the evidence rather than against fashion.
