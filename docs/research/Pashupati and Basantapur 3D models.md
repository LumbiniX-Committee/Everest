# Measure what's sourced, label everything else

Only a few dimensions for these buildings have sources, and several of those disagree. **Pashupatinath's main temple has a published overall height of about 23.6–23.7 m.** It is a square, two-tiered pagoda with gilded copper roofs, a gold finial, four silver-clad doors and a gilded Nandi at the west door. No source gives its footprint, plinth height or tier heights. In Kathmandu Durbar Square, **Kasthamandap is the only building with scholarly measured data**: Slusser and Korn give about 20 m square and about the same in height. **Taleju has a widely repeated 35 m height but no primary survey.** The nine-storey Basantapur tower has no sourced height at all. Footprints for Maju Dega (about 28 × 28 m), Gaddi Baithak, Trailokya Mohan and the Taleju plinth come from OpenStreetMap polygons. They are good for layout and weak for exact size. Every other figure must come from photographs, so the app has to show "estimate" as a visible state. If it doesn't, it breaks its own promises that a measurement is never faked and that "by eye" is never presented as measured. On content, the respectful approach is clear. Describe Pashupati in words only, as Lord Shiva worshipped as a four-faced lingam with a fifth, formless face turned upward. Never show the sanctum, and never make a deity into a mascot. The four mascots should be ordinary, contemporary people and a real animal, and each one needs specific details that stop it becoming a stereotype.

## Pashupatinath is sourced in height, materials and orientation, not in plan

The overall height of the main temple is the one number that sources repeat. Wikipedia gives "23 m 7 cm from base to pinnacle" on a square base, citing a secondary academia.edu paper rather than a survey ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashupatinath_Temple)). Guidebooks give **23.6 m (77.4 ft)** for a "two-tiered pagoda built on a single-tier plinth" ([The Longest Way Home](https://www.thelongestwayhome.com/travel-guides/nepal/guide-to-pashupatinath.html); [Sacred Destinations](https://www.sacred-destinations.com/nepal/kathmandu-pashupatinath)). Other figures run from 23 m to 24 m ([Nepal Traveller](https://nepaltraveller.com/sidetrack/unique-architectural-features-of-pashupatinath-temple); [bharatonline](https://www.bharatonline.com/nepal/tourist-attractions/kathmandu/pashupatinath.html)). The figures agree closely, but they look like one number copied between sources rather than independent measurements. Treat the height as **23.6 m ±0.5 m, published approximation, medium confidence**. One architecture guide calls its own figures "widely accepted approximations". It describes a whitewashed brick square base and two tapering roofs, broad and heavy at the bottom and small and light at the top, carried on rows of carved timber struts (tundal) ([Studio Matrx](https://www.studiomatrx.org/guides/pashupatinath-temple-architecture)).

The qualitative features are well supported. The temple has **two roofs of copper with gold covering**, a **gold pinnacle (gajur)**, and **four main doors, one on each side, covered in silver sheet** with gilt detail ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashupatinath_Temple); [The Longest Way Home](https://www.thelongestwayhome.com/travel-guides/nepal/guide-to-pashupatinath.html)). The first-tier struts carry carvings of Shiva's family, but no source gives the number of struts or their spacing. The present fabric dates from a **1692 renovation** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashupatinath_Temple)). It came through the 2015 earthquake essentially undamaged, while outer buildings in the complex were damaged ([Millennium Post](https://www.millenniumpost.in/pashupatinath-temple-unharmed-in-nepals-devastating-quake-62324)). The model should therefore show it intact and fully gilded. The Area Development Trust reserved gold for "the roof and the basaha" in 2021 ([Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/valley/2021/02/18/pashupati-area-development-trust-presses-ahead-with-golden-jalahari-installation-plan)). The gold jalahari, weighed at 107.468 kg, was reinstalled in June 2023 ([Republica](https://myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com/news/gold-jalhari-reinstalled-at-pashupatinath-temple)), but it sits inside the sanctum and must not be modelled. Two claims should stay out of the model: a single source's story that the temple once had three roofs, and an uncorroborated note about silver eave trim.

The large gilded Nandi (Basaha) stands in front of the **west door**, facing the sanctum. Its back is to the west gate, so non-Hindus see its "golden behind" through the gateway ([Lonely Planet](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/points-of-interest/nandi-statue/1508322)). Its size is weakly sourced. One blog says "about three meters long and five meters high" ([Nepal Heavenly Treasures](https://nepalheavenlytreasures.blogspot.com/2016/08/pashupatinath-temple.html)). A seated bull 3 m long cannot be 5 m tall, so that figure probably includes a plinth or swaps two numbers. Model it at **about 3 m long and 2–2.5 m high on a low plinth, labelled as an estimate**.

The precinct can be laid out schematically but not to survey accuracy. The main temple stands in a walled courtyard on the **west bank of the Bagmati**, entered from the west. Vasuki's small pagoda is in the **north-east corner** and the metal image of Unmatta Bhairav is to the south ([Nepal Heavenly Treasures](https://nepalheavenlytreasures.blogspot.com/2016/08/pashupatinath-temple.html)). The cremation ghats step down to the river; Arya Ghat hosts the Trust's 24-hour Kiryaputri service ([PADT](https://pashupati.org.np/?p=135)). On the east bank, the Trust lists **Pandra Shivalaya, fifteen miniature temples with Shiva lingas** ([PADT](https://pashupati.org.np/15-shivalaya-%E0%A5%A7%E0%A5%AB-%E0%A4%B6%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%B5%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B2%E0%A4%AF/)), with the wooded hill rising behind them. Vishwarupa, about 108 ft (33 m) tall, and the shikhara-style Gorakhnath are also on the hill ([PADT monuments](https://www.pashupati.org.np/?p=278)). The whole protected area is 246 ha ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashupatinath_Temple)). That is the heritage zone, not a modelling extent.

The access rules decide which view the app should treat as the honest default. Non-Hindus may not enter the main temple. The Trust says observation from the surrounding ghats is permitted and photography is not allowed inside the main sanctum ([PADT](https://www.pashupati.org.np/)). The Tourism Board adds that leather is forbidden in the complex ([NTB](https://ntb.gov.np/pashupatinath)). For most users, the default camera should be **the east-bank terrace looking west across the river**, with the courtyard interior shown as "Hindus only". Courtyard dimensions, wall heights and the exact positions of the shrines have no source, so the layout must be labelled "schematic, not surveyed".

## Durbar Square has one well-measured building and good OSM positions for the rest

**Kasthamandap** is the anchor. Slusser's 1974 study, with Wolfgang Korn's measured drawings, gives a breadth "just short of sixty-six feet" (**≈20.1 m**), "almost the same in height". The ground floor has **four central pillars, each 20 ft (6.1 m) tall and 15 in (0.38 m) square**, plus **32 peripheral columns**. The central pillars narrow to 10 in (0.25 m) on the second storey and 5 in (0.13 m) on the third, which has six columns a side. There are **four stairways, one per side**, corner pavilions on the porch, hipped tiled roofs and a gilt finial ([Slusser, asianart.com](https://asianart.com/articles/buildings/index.html)). A Durham paper puts the four central pillars in a square of just under **4.5 m** ([Durham](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/1375477)). A student presentation gives 18.7 × 18.73 m and 16.3 m high ([SlideShare](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/kasthamandap/231143415)). That gap may be building versus plinth, but it is unresolved, so Slusser should take precedence. The three-storey timber pavilion collapsed on 25 April 2015 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasthamandap)). It was rebuilt from Korn's drawings, with major structural work finished by December 2021 ([Nepali Times](https://nepalitimes.com/resurrecting-kasthamandap-from-the-rubble); [ShareSansar](https://www.sharesansar.com/newsdetail/rebuilding-of-kasthamandap-nears-final-phase-to-be-inaugurated-within-a-few-days-2021-11-23)). Because the rebuild followed the same drawings, the pre-2015 proportions are valid for the present building.

**Taleju** is usually given as **35 m** (one source says 36 m), the tallest temple in the Valley. It has three roofs on **12 plinth stages**, though one source instead describes a five-tiered base ([The Longest Way Home](https://thelongestwayhome.com/travel-guides/nepal/kathmandu/taleju-temple-kathmandu.html); [Onlinekhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/taleju-bhawani-in-kathmandu.html); [Himalayan Times](https://thehimalayantimes.com/entertainment/magnificence-of-a-shrine)). OSM maps its stepped base as a polygon of about **56 × 55 m (2,158 m²)** inside an enclosure of about 69 × 84 m. **Maju Dega** was built in 1692 by Riddhi Lakshmi. It is a three-roofed Shiva temple on a **nine-step brick base** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maju_Dega); [Lonely Planet](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/nepal/kathmandu/attractions/maju-deval/a/poi-sig/386989/357144)). OSM gives the plinth as **about 28 × 28 m**, which fits a nine-step base. The 2015 earthquake destroyed it, and reconstruction began on 29 October 2018 ([Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/valley/2018/10/29/mayor-shakya-inaugurates-reconstruction-of-maju-dega-temple-20181029144058)). A 2023 photograph still shows it behind sheeting ([ozoutback](https://www.ozoutback.com.au/Nepal/durbar/slides/15-20230915036.html)). Its present state must be confirmed on site before the app shows it as complete.

The 20th-century and residential buildings are sourced by date and use, not by size. **Gaddi Baithak** is the 1908 neoclassical hall built under Chandra Shumsher. It was restored and reopened in June 2018 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaddi_Baithak); [Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.ekantipur.com/news/2018-06-28/pm-oli-inaugurates-restored-historic-gaddi-baithak.html)). Its OSM polygon is about **48 × 45 m, three levels**, which probably includes attached wings. **Kumari Ghar** is a three-storey brick courtyard house from **1757** with carved windows, on the south side of the square ([Sacred Destinations](https://www.sacred-destinations.com/nepal/kathmandu-kumari-ghar); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathmandu_Durbar_Square)). OSM has it only as a point. The Kumari herself must never be photographed, and sources disagree on whether the courtyard may be ([Lonely Planet](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/points-of-interest/kumari-ghar-kumari-bahal/386969)). The model therefore needs no interior and should never include a figure at the window. **Trailokya Mohan Narayan** has three roofs ([Lonely Planet](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/points-of-interest/gaddhi-baithak/386885)), on an OSM footprint of about 15 × 15 m. The wording about it "surmounting" Gaddi Baithak conflicts with OSM, which places it about 50 m west, facing Kasthamandap. **Use the OSM position.**

The **Basantapur (nine-storey) tower** has the strongest historical sourcing and no physical dimensions. Lohan Chowk is a four-storey square courtyard with a tower at each corner. **Basantapur is the south-west tower and has nine storeys** ([China Daily](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202502/05/WS67a2a4daa310a2ab06eaa1a0_2.html)). The other three are the Kirtipur, Bhaktapur (Lakshmi Bilas) and Patan or Lalitpur (Bilas Mandir) towers, said to represent the Valley's four cities ([Lonely Planet](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/points-of-interest/lohan-chowk/1377902)). Prithvi Narayan Shah added them around 1768–1770. In 2015, by China Daily's account, the tower's **top three storeys** collapsed, along with the sixth storey of the north-east tower. Lonely Planet instead names the Bhaktapur tower as the second damaged tower ([Lonely Planet](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/nepal/kathmandu/attractions/hanuman-dhoka/a/poi-sig/386901/357144)). Chinese teams restored the complex from November 2016 with the stated aim of returning it to its original state. Handover came in August 2024 ([China Daily](https://govt.chinadaily.com.cn/s/202501/09/WS677f3d44498eec7e1f72ccd2/nepals-basantapur-tower-restored-after-eight-year-project.html)), and the Nautale Durbar reopened to the public in May 2025 ([Onlinekhabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/historic-nine-storey-palace-in-basantapur-reopens-to-the-public.html)). The present-day model should show **all nine storeys, restored**. A height of **28–35 m** comes only from assuming 2.8–3.5 m per storey. It is an estimate and must never be shown as a measurement.

The site plan is better constrained than the buildings. An OSM Overpass query run on 2026-11-11 gives centroids, in metres east and north of 27.7040 N, 85.3070 E:

Kasthamandap to Taleju is about **240 m**. The Basantapur plaza's extent is a coarse estimate of **100–130 m east–west by 50–70 m north–south**, taken from point positions. The plaza, the Hanuman Dhoka gate, Lohan Chowk and the Nautalle block have no OSM polygons and must be traced from imagery. **OSM height tags here are wrong** (Kasthamandap is tagged `height=1`) and must not be imported. Wikipedia's 167.37 ha is the heritage zone, not the plaza ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathmandu_Durbar_Square)).

For proportions with no source, the only sourced rules are qualitative. Newar temples have square plans and stepped plinths that shrink as they rise. They have 2–5 hipped roofs, each smaller than the one below, with a finial on top ([Wikipedia, Newar architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newar_architecture)). Their struts get smaller higher up ([Nepal Traveller](https://nepaltraveller.com/sidetrack/tundal-in-nepalese-architecture)). A "45° strut angle" and claimed Shilpa Shastra ratios found online were unsourced and look AI-generated, so they are excluded. The defaults in the specification table below are therefore labelled schematic: each roof about 70–80% of the width of the one below, and strut overhang about equal to strut length.

## Describe Pashupati in words, from the sanctum's own vocabulary

The Trust's own wording sets the tone. It refers to **"भगवान् पशुपतिनाथ" (Lord Pashupatinath) as the "आराध्यदेव"**, the deity who is worshipped ([PADT](https://www.pashupati.org.np/)). The Tourism Board calls the temple "the most revered Hindu temple in Nepal". It describes Maha Shivaratri, "the Night of Lord Shiva", as the night when pilgrims, sadhus and ascetics come to see "the sacred Shiva lingam" ([NTB](https://ntb.gov.np/pashupatinath)). Pashupati is Shiva "in his benign aspect as the five-faced herdsman of all creatures", generally rendered as "lord of animals" or "protector of all beings", and is described as Nepal's national deity ([Wikipedia: Pashupati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashupati)). The sanctum holds a **stone four-faced (chaturmukha) lingam about one metre high on a silver base** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashupatinath_Temple)). The faces are **Tatpurusha to the east, Aghora to the south, Sadyojata to the west and Vamadeva to the north**, and **Ishana, the fifth, is formless and faces upward** ([Wikiwand](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/Pashupatinath_Temple); [eSamskriti](https://esamskriti.com/e/Culture/Indian-Culture/Pashupatinath-Temple-Nepal--1.aspx)).

Recommended app text, built from that wording: *"Pashupatinath is the most revered Hindu temple in Nepal, dedicated to Lord Shiva as Pashupati, the Lord and protector of all living beings. In the sanctum, Shiva is worshipped as a lingam with four faces looking to the four directions and a fifth, formless face turned upward. The main temple is open to Hindus only; everyone is welcome on the ghats and the east-bank terraces."* Use the honorific "Lord", and avoid "idol". Do not reduce Shiva to "god of destruction". Two things should wait for a scholarly Shaiva reference, such as Axel Michaels's *Śiva in Trouble*, which could not be retrieved. One is the meaning attached to each face. The other is the link to the five elements, which appears only in text derived from Wikipedia. One outlier claim of "eight faces" is an error. Worship is led by **Bhatta priests, South Indian Brahmins, assisted by Rajbhandaris** ([Kamakoti Mandali](https://kamakotimandali.com/2021/03/31/pashupatinath-2)). How that tradition began is disputed between Shankaracharya, Yaksha Malla and a 1755 revival ([Kanak Mani Dixit](https://kanakmanidixit.com/?p=3497)), so the app should say "by tradition" and not pick one.

There are good reasons not to make a mascot of any deity. The Hindu American Foundation's 2019 guidelines list "any item with significant material modification of a Deity" as one that **may cause offence**, and say that context and intent matter ([HAF](https://www.hinduamerican.org/?p=2408)). Controversy has followed even soft products: Wayfair withdrew a deity beach towel in 2020 ([RNS](https://religionnews.com/2020/01/02/namaste-corporate-america-when-retail-and-reverence-collide/)), and a museum's Krishna plush toys split opinion in 2022 ([Las Vegas Sun](https://lasvegassun.com/news/2022/oct/18/activist-objects-to-museum-selling-toys-of-hindu-d/)). A mascot that users tap, animate and joke with is a stronger modification than a static image. Keeping deities out of mascots is not an explicit rule in any source. It is the defensible reading, and the same reasoning applies to Buddhist imagery. Mascots should point toward the temple, never carry or wear deity icons, and the app should show no image of the sanctum or the lingam, in line with the photography ban.

## Four mascots that read as people and an animal, not types

**The young Nepali guide** should look like a licensed professional, not a tout. The Department of Tourism licenses guides, and NATHM is the main trainer ([DoT](https://www.tourismdepartment.gov.np/files/publication_files/327.pdf)). Since 31 May 2023, trekkers in protected areas must hire a licensed guide ([ShareSansar](https://www.sharesansar.com/newsdetail/decision-requiring-trekkers-to-be-accompanied-by-licensed-guides-comes-into-effect-2023-06-02)). Agency sources say the card shows a name, photo, licence number and expiry ([Best Heritage Tour](https://www.bestheritagetour.com/blog/guide-vs-porter-guide)). A clipped or lanyard photo-ID is therefore a believable detail, but its text should be illegible or fictional and it **must not copy the government card design or emblem**. No source describes what urban heritage guides wear. The design direction here is unsourced observation: everyday outdoor clothes and a daypack, lighter clothes for the hot Terai around Lumbini, and a dhaka topi at most as a touch of pride. Full daura-suruwal every day reads as costume. Whether city heritage guides hold the same licence as trekking guides was not confirmed.

**The porter** should be named for the job, never as "Sherpa". Sherpa is an ethnic group, not an occupation ([Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.ekantipur.com/columns/2021/02/25/sherpas-should-step-out-of-the-shadows); [CNN](https://amp.cnn.com/cnn/travel/article/sherpa-facts)). If the character is Tamang, Rai, Gurung or Sherpa, the bio can say so as identity. The IPPG, formed in 1997, says porters must be given "clothing appropriate to season and altitude" ([IPPG guidelines reproduced](https://localladakhiguide.blogspot.com/2008/09/international-porter-protection-group.html)). Load caps are reported as 30 kg in Nepal ([Rural21](https://www.rural21.com/english/archive/2020/01/detail/article/everest-porters-the-backbone-of-trekking-burdened-by-unfair-wages-and-risks.html)) and lower on high routes elsewhere. So draw a **modest load, proper shoes and a warm jacket**. Never draw a comically towering pile: that gag hides exactly the welfare problem the IPPG campaigns on. The doko is a conical bamboo back-basket carried with a namlo forehead strap, and a T-stick can prop the load during rests. Those details are general knowledge, not fetched sources. A doko fits a market or village porter; trekking porters more often carry duffels tied with a namlo. Give the character expertise and humour, not just strength.

**The temple macaque** must be an accurate rhesus macaque (*Macaca mulatta*). That is the documented species at Swayambhu and Pashupati ([CDC EID 2006](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/eid/article/12/6/06-0030-t2)). Its look, from general primatology rather than a fetched source, is brown-grey fur with orange-tinged hindquarters, a bare pinkish face and a medium tail, not a cartoon chimp or capuchin. The monkeys are revered as connected with Hanuman ([Atlas Obscura](https://assets.atlasobscura.com/articles/2017)), so the character must carry **no Hanuman iconography**: no mace, crown or devotional pose. Two findings argue against a "thieving monkey" gag and against ever showing anyone feeding or touching it. Of 39 Swayambhu macaques tested, **64.1% carried B virus antibodies** ([CDC EID](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/eid/article/12/6/06-0030-t2)). A 250-hour study at Pashupati and Swayambhu found aggression mostly *responds* to human behaviour ([Nepalese Journal of Zoology](https://www.nepjol.info/index.php/njz/article/view/30870)). A good role for the macaque is a safety tip: keep food zipped away, don't stare. App text should say "Swayambhunath", not "Monkey Temple".

**The Newar woodcarver** should be a working professional of today. Carving belongs mainly to the **Shilpakar (Sikami)** community. The tools are the chisel (hāčā), round chisel (čapanchā), adze, handsaw, wooden mallet and jack plane, traditionally forged by Khami smiths ([KU dissertation](https://elibrary.ku.edu.np/bitstream/20.500.14301/400/1/Dissertation%20-%20Saroj%20Raj%20Gopsai%20final.pdf); [Hinduism Today](https://www.hinduismtoday.com/magazine/october-1999/1999-10-worship-in-wood/)). Typical pieces are windows, including the tikijhya lattice, toranas over doorways and temple struts ([GlobalInCH](https://www.globalinch.org/craft/craft-in-architecture-woodcarving/)). After 2015, Bhaktapur carvers were recruited to restore Patan, earning wages and training apprentices ([CS Monitor](https://m.csmonitor.com/World/Asia-South-Central/2017/0717/In-post-quake-rebuilding-Kathmandu-s-carvers-reclaim-a-fading-heritage)). That is the opposite of a "timeless ancient artisan". Show the carver working low to the ground with chisels, mallet and shavings, on a **lattice window or floral panel rather than a deity strut**. Avoid the "old man artisan" default; a woman carver is plausible. Dress the character in everyday workwear, with a black Bhaktapur-style topi for a man as an optional regional touch. Haku patasi is women's dress and must not go on a male carver. These dress details were not confirmed from a fetched source and need a Newar cultural reviewer.

## Conclusion

The biggest risk to accuracy is not a wrong number. It is a confident 3D model whose estimated proportions look exactly like its measured ones. A shaded, photoreal pagoda implies a survey, even when most of its dimensions came from a photo. For these buildings, the honest product is a **two-layer model**. The sourced layer covers overall height, number of tiers, door count, orientation and OSM positions. The estimated layer covers footprints, tier heights, plinth steps and strut layout, and it should be visibly marked, perhaps with a "schematic" badge or a different material. The same rule applies to time. Kasthamandap and the Basantapur tower are confidently "restored". Maju Dega's completion is unconfirmed and should be flagged as such, not assumed.

One step would turn most "estimate" rows below into "sourced": get Korn's *Traditional Architecture of the Kathmandu Valley*, the *Paśupatikṣetra* inventory plates (Michaels, Tandon and Fritzenkötter, 2017), Gutschow's Heidelberg drawings, and DANAM's per-monument reports through a library. Until then, the honest choice is to show the gaps. A guide app that says "we know the height, the plan is estimated" models the same respect for evidence that its content asks visitors to show the sacred sites.

Confidence is High (scholarly, official, or several independent sources), Medium (secondary sources that agree), Low (a single weak source or an OSM footprint), or Estimate (derived; show it as estimated in the app). OSM positions are metres east and north of 27.7040 N, 85.3070 E.
