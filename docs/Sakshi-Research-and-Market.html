What the literature already establishes, who else is in the field, and how large the opportunity actually is.

The claim this report has to support

Sākṣī asserts that ordinary visitors to a heritage site, given a fixed viewpoint and an honest instrument, can produce a monitoring record a conservator will actually use — and that pilgrimage is a large enough and reliable enough flow of people to keep that record current at national scale. That is four separable claims, and the literature speaks to each.

The pattern is worth stating plainly: the two halves of the product rest on very different ground. The witness loop stands on settled method, and its risk is operational — recruitment, retention, siting. The AI stands on a literature two to three years old, and its risk is technical, which is why every AI output in the product is designed to be a proposal that a person disposes of.

Repeat photography and citizen science

Repeat photography is not an innovation of this project; it is an inheritance. Landscape and heritage science have used fixed-viewpoint image pairs for well over a century, and the heritage-specific review literature now covers the whole chain — image registration technique, archival organisation and retrieval, presentation of the comparison, and the conservation of rephotographic collections as heritage in their own right.2 The rePhotoSA project in southern Africa is the closest working model for the distribution half of the idea: an open, internet-based citizen science programme built on historical landscape photographs, which reports that repeat imagery is unusually effective at communicating landscape change to a lay public — a finding that matters as much to the product's growth as to its science.1

The data-quality question is the one every institutional buyer asks first, and it has been answered quantitatively. The Snapshot Serengeti analysis remains the reference result: aggregated volunteer classifications agreed with expert-verified data on 98% of images, and bootstrapping showed 90% of images were correctly classified with just five volunteers each — while accuracy varied systematically with how common the subject was, rare cases carrying higher false-positive and false-negative rates.6 The remedy is structural rather than exhortative: redundancy plus aggregation, with user-weighted consensus that dilutes inconsistent classifiers without banning them.5,6 Zooniverse operates this at scale across more than 120 projects and over two million registered volunteers, taking roughly 100,000 classifications a day.5

The heritage-specific participation study — Snap Decisions, on a park photo-monitoring programme — is the closest analogue to the Sākṣī loop and the most useful corrective: it asks not only whether the data is good but whether people keep showing up, the variable that decides whether a series exists in year five.3

What the literature tells us to build

Three design consequences follow, and all three are already in the product: redundancy at the vantage, because a series with several observers per viewpoint is measurably more trustworthy than one with a single expert; uncertainty carried on the record, because volunteer data is reliable in aggregate and must be able to say when it is not; and a rare-case bias to watch, because the unusual condition — the one worth reporting — is where non-expert accuracy is weakest, and so the one place a custodian's confirmation must be mandatory.

Recent research bearing directly on the build

3.1  Damage detection on heritage surfaces

Crack detection has moved from a research curiosity to a reviewable field in about three years. A 2026 systematic review of deep learning for crack detection in cultural heritage analysed 26 papers published between 2020 and 2025.7 The architectural picture is clear: attention-augmented U-Net variants such as BSEResU-Net for segmentation, and the YOLO family — v8 through v11 — for detection, including field deployments such as St. Theodore Church in Cappadocia.7,9 A 2025 two-stage framework pairing classification with semantic segmentation is positioned as a lightweight option for large-scale heritage inspection,10 and 2026 work reports YOLOv8n-Ghost variants for on-device damage segmentation at Italian historical sites — the same bet this product makes.9

The honest caveat is generalisation. A 2025 review of learning paradigms, generalisability and datasets for crack detection is largely a catalogue of how poorly models transfer across materials, lighting and capture geometry.8 Brick, coursed stone, lime plaster and eroded sandstone do not share a crack morphology, and a model trained on concrete infrastructure will underperform on a Malla-era brick façade. This is the strongest argument in the literature for the product's own rule that the detector proposes and a person disposes, and for treating confirmed reports as the training set that closes the loop.

3.2  Digital twins, HBIM, and where a photograph fits

A scoping review of 204 peer-reviewed studies from 2020–2025 finds the digital-twin heritage stack settling on terrestrial laser scanning, UAV photogrammetry, BIM and IoT sensor networks, with structure-from-motion photogrammetry established as the cost-effective alternative to laser scanning in confined or complex settings.11,12

This is the most important competitive reading in the report, and it cuts in our favour. The digital-twin literature is capital-intensive by construction — it assumes a scanning campaign, a specialist crew, and a periodic budget. It produces exquisite geometry at intervals of years. Sākṣī produces coarser evidence continuously, at effectively zero marginal cost, from people who were going to visit anyway. Photogrammetry-driven digital twinning of time-varying change12 is precisely the workflow a dense corpus of fixed-viewpoint, pose-tagged visitor photographs feeds. The two are complements, and the honest positioning is that Sākṣī is the sampling layer beneath the twin, not a competitor to it.

3.3  Grounded generation, measured rather than asserted

Between 2024 and 2026 the faithfulness of retrieval-augmented answers became a measurable engineering property. RAGTruth supplies nearly 18,000 annotated examples with both response-level and span-level hallucination labels;15 evolving public leaderboards track hallucination rates across models using dedicated detection models;14 empirical work on groundedness in long-form generation16 and survey work on trustworthiness in RAG systems17 have established the vocabulary, and 2025 work such as ReDeEP approaches detection through mechanistic interpretability rather than output comparison alone.18 The finding that matters most to this product is the least comfortable one: models still introduce unsupported statements even when the correct context is in front of them. An architecture that merely retrieves is not an architecture that is grounded, which is why the verify-then-refuse step is not optional here.

3.4  Running models on the phone

The on-device inference literature has converged on the conclusion the product reached in practice. ExecuTorch reached 1.0 in October 2025 as a unified PyTorch on-device solution with a small runtime and broad vendor-backend coverage;19 systematic reviews compare llama.cpp, MLC LLM, ExecuTorch and ONNX Runtime Mobile on portability, throughput and deployment complexity;21 and work on NPU-targeted inference and on-device GPU scaling maps the performance ceiling on current handsets.20,22 Models in the 1–4 billion parameter range are now credible for constrained tasks — exactly the task Sākṣī assigns them: rephrasing a retrieved passage, never asserting a fact.

Competitive landscape

There is no direct competitor. There are four well-populated adjacent categories, each of which owns one segment of the chain that runs from a visitor's attention to a custodian's work order — and each of which stops before the next.

A.  Photo-monitoring citizen science Chronolog · rePhotoSA · Zooniverse · iNaturalist

The closest analogue by mechanism. Chronolog is the benchmark: fixed photo stations in parks, nature centres, schools and museums, where a visitor takes a photograph and submits it by email to build a crowdsourced time-lapse. Since 2017 it has operated across 48 US states and 10 countries and gathered more than 100,000 submissions.23

The gap:

Environmental, not heritage. A fixed bracket rather than a sensor-verified alignment, so there is no pose, no accuracy figure, no provenance a conservator could defend — and nothing downstream: the output is a time-lapse, not a report that reaches whoever is responsible for the site.

B.  Heritage inventory and management platforms Arches (Getty Conservation Institute + World Monuments Fund) · CollectiveAccess · CollectionSpace · Omeka S · Dédalo

Where the institutional record actually lives. Arches is the standard for immovable heritage: an open-source platform for inventorying type, location, extent, period, materials and condition, and the relationships between resources.24 The others serve museum and archival collections rather than sites.

The gap:

These are systems of record, and excellent ones, but they do not acquire. Arches waits for a professional survey to be entered into it; nothing turns a million annual visitors into dated, positioned condition observations. A partner, not a rival — the long-run posture is that Sākṣī feeds Arches.

C.  Field data capture and mobile GIS Esri ArcGIS Field Maps & Survey123 · Fulcrum · KoboToolbox · ODK · Mergin Maps

What a heritage office would otherwise buy. Generic, capable, and priced per seat — Esri's field apps come with ArcGIS Online from roughly US$125 per user per year, Fulcrum from about US$25 per user per month, while KoboToolbox and ODK are free and open source.25

The gap:

Per-seat pricing presumes a small trained crew, which structurally forbids the public from contributing. These tools also have no concept of a vantage: they will record where you were, not whether you stood where the last photograph was taken from — and that single distinction is the difference between a photo archive and a time-series.

D.  Visitor engagement and interpretation Smartify · Bloomberg Connects · Google Arts & Culture · izi.TRAVEL · CyArk

Where the funded activity is. Smartify raised £1.5M in January 2025 led by Metavallon VC, reporting around 2 million users, partnerships with over 700 organisations, and 68% usage growth year on year.26 Bloomberg Connects carries digital guides for more than 1,250 institutions.27

The gap:

Interpretation flows one way. These products deliver content to a visitor and take nothing back but analytics. The visitor is an audience, and the site gets no data about its own condition from having been visited.

Where Sākṣī sits

The chain is visitor attention → positioned evidence → institutional action. Category D owns the first link and stops. Category A bridges the first two for ecology, without survey-grade provenance. Category C serves the second link for professionals only, at a price that excludes the public. Category B owns the third and waits to be fed.

No product in the survey spans all three, and the two capabilities that make spanning them possible — sensor-verified alignment to a stored vantage, and a custodian surface that acknowledges and closes a report — are the two the adjacent categories each lack. The defensible position is not a feature; it is the loop.

These are the numbers a pitch quotes, and they are the wrong denominator. Sākṣī does not sell trips; it sells the ability to monitor the assets trips are sold against, and that money sits in conservation and public-heritage budgets, not tourist spend. The tourism figure matters for one reason, and it is the important one: it sizes the distribution channel. A market growing at five percent a year is a growing flow of people walking past monuments with phones in their hands, and that flow is the raw material.

The urgency is measurable, and it is not ours to argue

The demand side is no longer speculative. A 2025 assessment in Communications Earth & Environment found 80% of World Cultural Heritage sites under climate stress, with nearly one in five built substantially of threatened materials such as stone and wood, and reported that 98% of sites have faced at least one climate-related extreme since 2000; exposure could triple with a further 1 °C of warming by 2050, while a low-emission path would protect roughly 40% of threatened sites.32,33 A separate UNESCO assessment in July 2025 put close to three-quarters of World Heritage sites at high risk from water-related hazards, and UNESCO has since launched Sites Navigator, a live monitoring platform integrating more than 40 datasets with near-real-time alerts.34

That last development is the single most encouraging signal in this report. The institutions have built the dashboard and are short of the ground truth to fill it. Satellite and climate-model data can say a site is exposed; only someone standing in front of the wall can say whether the wall has moved.

What funds it, and what does not

There is no consumer subscription here, and pretending otherwise would misread every number above. The surfaces that fit are institutional licensing of the custodian platform to site trusts and heritage departments; grant and multilateral funding attached to climate-resilience and World Heritage monitoring, which is where the 2025 assessments have moved money; and paid integration into systems of record such as Arches. The ethics policy forecloses one alternative deliberately: no money from any commercial entity operating inside a site the app monitors, because a monitoring record whose funder has an interest in the finding is not a record.

Risks worth stating

Participation decay

The literature's clearest warning.3 A series needs returning observers, and novelty does not return. Mitigation is siting quests at the vantages that have gone longest without a resurvey, rather than at the popular ones.

Model generalisation

Crack detectors transfer poorly across materials.8 Mitigated structurally: the detector never sets severity, and confirmed reports become the training set.

Custodian capacity

Evidence a custodian cannot act on becomes evidence they stop reading. The acknowledgement workflow, and the median-time-to-acknowledgement metric on the dashboard, exist to make that visible early.

Institutional procurement

Heritage bodies buy slowly. The open-source and no-login posture lowers the barrier to a first deployment, but the sales cycle is measured in budget years.
