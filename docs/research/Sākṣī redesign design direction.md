# Make Sākṣī calm, sourced and offline-first

Sākṣī should look premium by showing restraint and showing where its information comes from. It should not chase brand ornament. The evidence points to a calm, editorial, photo-led interface that is built from local materials. In that interface every claim shows its source, every unknown is shown honestly, and every screen still works offline, in sunlight, on a budget Xiaomi phone. Leaders in this category did not reinvent their products in 2025. Komoot and AllTrails put their craft into content: real photos, purpose-built maps, and the go/no-go facts placed at the top. Airbnb's expensive brand-layer craft took years to build, and critics still called it decorative. For Sākṣī the differentiator is also the trust mechanism. Research shows that citations raise user trust **even when they are random**, so Ask is only honest if every citation can be checked in one tap. For Report, people whose first report gets fixed are **54–57% more likely to report again**, so visible, honest status counts for more than any visual polish. On culture, Kenzo Tange's Lumbini master plan already provides a sourced design brief: "tranquility, universality and extreme clarity", with nothing "that attract[s] the eye" away from the sacred subject. There is also a clear rule from recent controversies: sacred figures and symbols are never decoration. The physical constraints set the floor. Nepal is 4G-majority but speeds are too inconsistent to rank, sunlight cuts on-screen contrast sharply, and Devanagari needs more line height than Latin. The ranked principles below turn this into decisions for both the Next.js site and the Expo app.

## Ten principles, ranked by impact

The ranking weighs three things: how many users a principle affects, whether getting it wrong blocks use or breaks one of the five promises, and how hard the mistake is to undo. Cultural and trust failures rank high because they damage the brand and are hard to repair. Polish ranks low.

| Rank | Principle | Applies to | Why it ranks here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Make provenance visible and checkable everywhere**: one-tap citation passages, "measured" vs "by eye" tags, "not recorded" in place of 0, a sources block on every place | App + site | It is the product's reason to exist, and it enforces promises 1–3 |
| 2 | **Offline-first on a Redmi A-class phone**: bundled core content, queued writes, no spinner-gated screens | App | Without it, the app does not work in monument grounds or on treks |
| 3 | **Legible in sunlight, at 200% text size, in two scripts**: ≥7:1 primary text, 48dp targets, Devanagari line-height ≥1.5 | App + site | Outdoor use is the main use case |
| 4 | **Sacred subjects are content, never ornament**: no Buddha, eyes, Om, Kumari or prayer flags as UI decoration | App + site | Nepal has had a public removal over this. The damage is reputational and hard to reverse |
| 5 | **Honest status for Report**: a timeline that keeps "Fixed" separate from "Closed (reason)", with the first report's outcome treated as most important | App + public log | This is the strongest participation lever in the civic-tech evidence |
| 6 | **One depth control, not nested expanders**: Glance · Story · Scholar | App + place pages | NN/g finds usability drops beyond two disclosure levels |
| 7 | **Accuracy-gated, non-modal arrival**: "Near X?" when unsure, a chooser for ambiguous places, a manual "I'm here" | App | It turns GPS noise into trust instead of annoyance |
| 8 | **A material-led, place-derived visual system**: brick, lime wash, water and terai green; a serif display face; desaturated maps | App + site | This is what reads as premium and non-generic |
| 9 | **A landing page built as "one place, three verbs"**: a static hero showing a real screen, a sources section, per-place SEO pages | Site | It drives conversion, credibility and organic reach |
| 10 | **Evolve, don't reinvent; avoid AI-slop tells** | Both | It protects trust during the redesign itself |

## Premium in 2025 means restrained surfaces and crafted content

The two best-documented redesigns of 2025 both moved toward warmer, softer, photo-led interfaces, and both stressed continuity. Komoot refreshed its colours, fonts, icons, spacing and contrast but **kept its core navigation and workflows unchanged**. It based the redesign on 110 in-person interviews and more than 3,000 surveys, and **over 80% of users preferred the new design**, with another 15% liking it equally ([Komoot newsroom](https://newsroom.komoot.com/254263-komoot-unveils-modern-design-as-part-of-ambitious-product-roadmap/)). Komoot made photos first-class because users "frequently rely on photo content to choose or build their route." Airbnb went the other way and put its craft into the brand layer. It uses dimensional, animated icons that were "built up gradually over the last couple of years," clean white backgrounds and bold type ([It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/airbnb-app-redesign-140525)), and critics dismissed the icons as "skeuo-pastiche", meaning depth that adds no usability ([MIdST Labs](https://midstlabs.substack.com/p/skin-deep-human-centered-design)). For a small team building a heritage and trekking guide, the Komoot and AllTrails path is the right one. It invests in content craft, such as real photography, a purpose-built map and a well-ordered detail page, rather than a bespoke 3D icon system. It also argues for keeping Sākṣī's navigation stable and changing the surface first.

The detail page is where premium either appears or fails. AllTrails overhauled more than 400,000 trail pages in June 2024. It made length, format and estimated duration more prominent and added a photo gallery, a 3D route, current conditions and an **AI review summary labelled as AI-generated**, with real reviews beneath it ([AllTrails press](https://www.alltrails.com/press/alltrails-reveals-major-update)). Coverage notes that the go/no-go facts now sit at the top, visible without scrolling ([T3](https://www.t3.com/news/latest-alltrails-software-update-adds-ton-of-new-features-my-favourite-feature-is-the-mosquito-indicator)). The order recommended for a Sākṣī place page is: a full-bleed, credited real photograph; the name and a one-line identity (era, tradition, region); a key-facts row of three or four figures in tabular numerals; a map or route preview; the depth-controlled narrative; a sources block; and community content last. For treks, the facts row carries duration, altitude gain, maximum altitude and permit need. Any value that has not been measured shows "not recorded", never 0, which is promise 1 made visible. AllTrails' photo tour, which orders community photos by place along the route, maps directly onto a pilgrimage circuit.

Maps follow the same logic. Komoot's original map was designed with cartographers and colour-codes way types and contours, "and yet the map stays clean-looking" ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/3052410/how-komoot-built-the-best-bike-route-mapping-app/)). Gaia rebuilt its topo layer from scratch for backcountry use ([Gaia GPS blog](https://pigeon-hexahedron.squarespace.com/blog/the-new-and-much-improved-gaia-topo-map-layer)). Organic Maps, Komoot and Gaia all treat **offline** as a core feature ([OSM wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Organic_Maps)). In practice this means several things. Desaturate the basemap so that only Sākṣī's content carries colour: the route, the place pins and the user's position. Hide shops and fuel on heritage maps. Use hillshade and contours for treks and a near-flat light map for the Sacred Garden. Design pins as one shape with category glyphs, never default teardrops.

The "cheap" tells are just as well catalogued. They are mostly practitioner opinion rather than research, but they are consistent: a blue-to-purple gradient, Inter as the only typeface, a generic centred hero, three identical cards, identical fade-ins on every element, synthetic imagery, no real proof, and empty superlatives ([Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/built-with-ai-trust-signals-2026); [925 Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-web-design-guide)). In travel these combine with over-polished stock photos, and that matters because almost 2 in 3 travellers dig through user photos for "a more honest look", while only 8% trust professional photos most. That figure comes from a non-peer-reviewed insurer survey ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2025/08/06/vacationers-sceptical-about-picture-perfect-travel-photos-study-says)). Sākṣī should prefer real, dated, credited photography. In the app it should separate list items with spacing and hairlines and save cards for objects that can actually be tapped.

## Trust is the interface: depth, arrival, Ask and Report

### Progressive depth works as one remembered setting

NN/g's progressive disclosure guidance makes two firm points. Designs with **more than two disclosure levels "typically have low usability"**, and the control that reveals more must carry strong "information scent" so users can predict what they will get ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)). Building quick, medium and deep as summary → "more" → "even more" would break that rule. The pattern that fits is a **single segmented control with three labelled options, Glance · Story · Scholar**. It sets a depth preference that carries across places and swaps the content in place, so each screen shows exactly one level. The labels should promise time and substance ("30 sec", "3-min read", "sources and debate") rather than abstract tiers. Glance holds what every visitor needs: what this is, why it matters, where to look, and how to behave. Etiquette or safety notes such as "shoes off" must never sit inside a collapsed section. Within Scholar, topics like Architecture or Inscriptions can be single-level accordions, never nested, and they must be keyboard- and screen-reader-operable ([Basis design system](https://design.basis.com/components/accordion)). No controlled study compares segmented controls, sliders and expanders for choosing depth, so test this with real visitors.

### Arrival should admit what GPS doesn't know

Geotriggered-tour tools agree on four safeguards. They use trigger radii of at least about 20 m, because smaller zones fail from GPS "bounce" ([VoiceMap](https://docs.voicemap.me/tour-publishers/choosing-locations/)). They discard fixes worse than about 25 m. They add a deadband (hysteresis) so the status does not flicker at the boundary. And they offer manual activation when accuracy is poor ([GeoXp](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@mezzo-forte/geoxp)). Apple's location-privacy guidance shows approximate location as a shaded area rather than a falsely precise dot, and it asks apps to do as much as possible at low precision ([Apple WWDC20](https://developer.apple.com/wwdc20/10162)). Microsoft's HAX guideline 2, "Make clear how well the system can do what it can do," applies directly ([Amershi et al., CHI 2019](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/)). Sākṣī's arrival moment should therefore follow four rules. It is a **non-modal bottom sheet or banner**, never an interruption, and never auto-plays audio unless the user has started a tour. When accuracy is worse than the trigger radius it says "Near Maya Devi Temple?" with a confirm action. When the accuracy circle overlaps several places it shows a distance-ordered chooser with a "Not here" escape. It always offers a manual "I'm here" or list pick. Background arrival notifications should be opt-in, rate-limited, and tied to a tour the user started. The recent commit that decides monument presence with hysteresis is the right foundation, and the UI should show that honesty rather than hide it.

### Citations inflate trust, so make them cheap to check

This is the most important interaction finding in the research. In Ding et al. (AAAI 2025), trust in LLM answers rose significantly when citations were present, **even when the citations were random**, and fell when participants actually checked them ([AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/34550)). A pilot study found that verbalised uncertainty made users check citations more often, and that when they verified, how much they relied on an answer tracked how reliable it actually was ([KAIST HCAI workshop](https://xai.kaist.ac.kr/static/files/2025_hcai_workshop/paper_29.pdf)). A MIT preprint seen only through a third-party summary reports that even invalid links reduced critical evaluation ([alphaXiv](https://alphaxiv.org/overview/2504.06435v1)). The design consequence is that a citation chip only counts as grounding if checking it costs one tap. **Tapping a numbered marker should open the exact supporting passage in a bottom sheet**, with source title, author or institution, and date. A bare link or title is not enough. Markers should be per-claim, in the Perplexity style, rather than an AI Overviews-style list at the end ([index.dev](https://www.index.dev/blog/perplexity-vs-google-ai-overviews)). They should still stay light, because CHI 2026's PaperTrail found that claim-level evidence mapping helped users but cluttered the interface under time pressure ([arXiv 2602.21045](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.21045v1)). Mark only the sourced claims, leave unsourced sentences visibly unmarked, and close with a compact "Sources (3)" row.

Show confidence in words and only when it changes the decision: "Sources agree", "Sources disagree on the date", "Only one source". Do not attach a percentage to every answer. This follows Google PAIR's framing of explanation and graceful failure as the basis of calibrated trust ([PAIR Explainability + Trust](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/People%20+%20AI%20Guidebook%20-%20Explainability%20+%20Trust.pdf); [PAIR Errors + Graceful Failure](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/People%20+%20AI%20Guidebook%20-%20Errors%20+%20Graceful%20Failure.pdf)). "I don't know" should be a designed state, not a disclaimer attached to a made-up answer. It says what the app lacks, shows what it does have ("Here's what the guide says about the Ashoka Pillar"), and offers a next step. Uncertainty cues lower trust, and for a weak answer that is the intended result: the goal is calibration, not maximum trust. Label AI-generated text visibly, as AllTrails labels its review summary. That makes promise 3, "the AI suggests, it never decides," visible to users.

### Report wins on whether the first report gets resolved

The strongest civic-tech evidence comes from nearly 400,000 FixMyStreet reports. People whose first report was fixed were **54% more likely to report again** ([mySociety](https://www.mysociety.org/2015/03/10/local-government-you-can-shape-your-own-responsible-citizens/)), and the academic paper on the same data gives **57%** ([Oxford Research Archive](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:2bd169fd-0b27-46e4-8b81-1ccfccf7c55a/files/ma5b19b3d0c0005e21d0bc3e457691bf5)). A peer-reviewed Sacramento 311 study found that digital transparency increases participation, but the effect is **short-lived** ([NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10527224)). FixMyStreet's status model has the key distinction: "fixed" is set by staff, while "closed" can carry a reason such as duplicate, not responsible or no further action, without implying a repair ([FixMyStreet admin manual](https://fixmystreet.org/running/admin_manual/)). Sākṣī's Report flow follows from this. It runs photo → location (pre-filled, showing the accuracy circle and a draggable pin) → short category list plus "Other" → optional note → submit, works offline, and sends when connected. The reporter's view is a **timestamped timeline**: Received → Acknowledged → In progress → **Fixed** or **Closed (reason)**. Each step carries a note or an "after" photo from whoever acted. The status stays "Received" until someone actually acts, because drifting to "In progress" would be a faked measurement under promise 1. Duplicates link to the canonical report and are never deleted, which keeps promise 4. Because the transparency effect fades, a persistent "My reports" list and a map of nearby resolved issues matter more than a one-off confirmation screen. Public reports should hide the reporter's name by default and strip EXIF location data. That is a recommendation; FixMyStreet's own privacy controls were not verified.

## The landing page should walk one place through three verbs

Attention data should set the page structure. NN/g's eyetracking found **57% of viewing time above the fold** and **74% in the first two screenfuls** ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/)). Hero format is less settled than it seems: A/B tests of video against a static image are mixed, and a static image sometimes wins ([VWO](https://vwo.com/blog/image-outperforms-video-increases-monthly-revenue/)). Carousels clearly fail. Roughly 1% of visitors interact with them and 84% of those clicks land on slide one, and in a Conversionista test a static first slide got 40% clicks against 2% for the rotating carousel ([Ramotion](https://www.ramotion.com/blog/website-carousel-best-practices/)). Rotating Guide, Ask and Report through a hero would effectively hide two of the three pillars. The best leaders do not use their hero to show off the app. AllTrails uses a lifestyle trail photo, a search box and three task CTAs, and saves phone visuals for the feature blocks below ([alltrails.com](https://www.alltrails.com/)). Komoot uses a plain promise and a signup button ([komoot.com](https://www.komoot.com/)).

The recommended Sākṣī hero is a **static "live answer"**: a credited photograph of a named Lumbini monument beside a flattened phone screenshot of a real Guide or Ask screen for that monument, plus one specific headline that a competitor could not also claim. "Know the story of every stone in Lumbini" passes that test; "Your AI travel companion" fails it ([Lean Labs](https://www.leanlabs.com/faq/b2b-websites/why-saying-were-better-or-were-different-doesnt-work)). A scripted Ask demo sits just below the fold, lazy-loaded so it costs nothing at LCP. It uses pre-written questions with real cached answers and tappable citations, because showing grounding is more convincing than claiming it. Copy should stay plain. In Unbounce's 2024 benchmark, pages written at a 5th–7th grade reading level converted at **11.1%**, against 5.3% for professional-level copy, and SaaS pages of 250–725 words did best ([PR Newswire](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/unbounces-2024-conversion-benchmark-report-proves-that-attention-spans-are-declining-and-so-are-conversion-rates-302239407.html); [Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report/saas-conversion-rate/)). Both results are correlational.

The section order adapts the AllTrails and Komoot pattern:

1. Hero.
2. "One place, three verbs". A single monument walks through Guide (the depth control with real text), Ask (a real question with citation chips) and Report (a broken signboard moving from reported to fixed, with dates).
3. Three short pillar blocks, each with a real screen and a verb-plus-plain-subtitle heading: "Ask: answers that show their sources".
4. **"Where the answers come from"**: the named sources and the method, plus the five promises stated plainly.
5. Places covered, as indexable per-monument and per-trek pages.
6. Who built it, with names, a location in Nepal and a contact address.
7. Final CTA with APK help.
8. Footer.

This order also answers the credibility problem of an app with no reviews yet. NN/g names design quality, upfront disclosure, correct and current content, and connection to the rest of the web as the trust factors ([NN/g](https://nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/)). AllTrails carries **no popularity numbers on its homepage** and relies on values-based affiliations instead. Never fake logos or quotes. Avoid copy that implies nobody has used the app, because negative social proof backfires ([Convince & Convert](https://www.convinceandconvert.com/digital-marketing/negative-social-proof/)). Name the sources you cite, but do not present them as partners without their permission.

Distribution details matter too. Use the official badges unmodified, with one App Store badge per layout "in a subordinate position to the image or main message" ([Apple guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/marketing/guidelines)), and the "Get it on Google Play" badge, localised where possible ([Google Play badges](https://play.google.com/intl/ar/badges/)). Follow AllTrails in pairing a **QR code with the badges in each feature block on desktop**. On mobile, hide the QR code and lead with the badge for the visitor's OS. Never show a full-screen install interstitial, which Google treats as a mobile ranking negative ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/interstitialgeddon-google-warns-will-crack-intrusive-interstitials-next-january-257252)). Android developer verification began on **30 September 2026** in its first countries and will apply globally to certified devices in **2027**. The advanced path for unverified apps involves a 24-hour wait and warnings ([Google support](https://support.google.com/android-developer-console/answer/16561738?hl=en); [TechRepublic](https://techrepublic.com/article/news-google-android-sideloading-24-hour-wait)). Ship the APK from a verified developer account and say so on the page. Also publish the SHA-256 checksum, a three-step install guide, and the permissions requested with the reason for each.

## Lumbini's own master plan is the culturally sourced brief

Nepal already has two useful precedents. Imageark's Visit Nepal identity deliberately chose yellow to **escape the red and blue typical of Nepal tourism promotion**, and framed Nepal as "much more than Everest and trekking" ([Online Khabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/the-inside-story-of-branding-new-nepal.html); [Online Khabar](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/branding-new-nepal.html)). That gives local backing for an unexpected but Nepal-rooted palette. The Lumbini master plan supplies the governing values. The Allchin–Matsushita report named "tranquility, universality and extreme clarity" as the essence of the Sacred Garden, and Tange wanted the surroundings "as simple and normal as possible without making gardens etc. that attract the eye" ([Nepali Times archive](https://archive.nepalitimes.com/news.php?id=9848); [Heidelberg repository](https://fid4sa-repository.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/3410/1/Lumbini.pdf)). His plan uses a north–south axis and canal so that pilgrims have time to prepare as they approach ([Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/05/02/lumbini-master-plan-remains-unfinished-after-48-years)). This reads directly as a UI brief: low ornament, the place as the subject, and a navigation metaphor of **approach and arrival** rather than a busy dashboard. Because Sākṣī now covers treks and "any place", generalise the principle rather than the palette. Each place's material, whether brick, lime wash, stone, sal forest or monsoon green, supplies its accents over a neutral, quiet system. That avoids defaulting to saffron and gold, a combination that also inverts paubha practice, where gold is reserved for divine faces and hands ([Himalayan Mart](https://himalayanmart.com/blogs/news/paubha-painting-the-sacred-newari-art-of-nepal)).

The firmest cultural rule is about sacred imagery. Buddhist bodies hold that Buddha images "are not meant to be used for decoration... under any circumstance" ([Knowing Buddha](https://knowingbuddha.org/another-successful-cooperation-with-a-sense-of-respect/)). Commercial misuse has led to deportation and protests ([Coconuts Bangkok](https://coconuts.co/bangkok/features/tattoos-toilet-seats-misused-buddha-images-make-blood-boil)). Nepal's own example is the Visit Nepal 2020 yeti statues: the Indra Jatra Organising Committee called the Kumari imagery on them "very insensitive", and **20 statues were removed** ([Mark Horrell](https://www.markhorrell.com/blog/2020/much-ado-about-yetis-nepals-latest-tourism-blunder/)). The rule for Sākṣī is that sacred subjects appear **documentarily, with context, never as ornament**. That rules out Buddha heads as icons, Swayambhu eyes as a logo or "watching" motif, Om as a bullet point, mandalas as loading spinners, and prayer flags as borders, progress bars or tab strips. Flags are customarily kept off the ground ([tnp.org](https://tnp.org/5-things-to-know-about-tibetan-prayer-flags/)), and a footer is the UI's ground. Images of deities should never be cropped at the face, overlaid with UI, or used as collectible grid thumbnails. Show people by name and role rather than as scenery. Western narratives have made Himalayan people "invincible and invisible," and "Sherpa" is an ethnic group, not a job title ([Lithub](https://lithub.com/for-too-long-we-have-only-known-western-stories-of-the-himalayas/)). Photography rules at sacred sites deserve UI support. At Pashupatinath, for example, the inner sanctum and rites are off-limits to cameras, and guidance says "When in doubt, do not take the picture" ([Wego](https://blog.wego.com/pashupatinath-temple-visitor-guide/)). Report's camera should warn at sanctums and must never ask for photos of worshippers or cremations. ICOM's revised Code of Ethics, adopted in June 2026, likewise asks institutions to recognise the "sacred significance and sensitivity of heritage" in how they interpret and display it ([CIMAM](https://cimam.org/news-archive/icom-adopts-its-revised-code-of-ethics-for-museums/); [ICOM draft](https://icom.museum/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Revised-ICOM-Code-of-Ethics-First-Draft.pdf)).

Script is the most valuable culturally authentic material, and it is not costume. Treat Devanagari as first-class content using a UI-designed face. Hind was "developed explicitly for use in User Interface design" ([Google Fonts](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Hind/about)), and Noto Sans Devanagari UI is another option ([Figma Fonts](https://www.figma.com/fonts/noto-sans-devanagari-ui/)). Give Devanagari **its own line-height token**, because "it is unwise to try and match line-height values to the Latin text" ([Mark Simonson](https://www.marksimonson.com/fonts/view/proxima-nova/)). A practitioner starting point is about 150–160% for body text ([IndiaFont](https://indiafont.com/blog/indian-typography-tips)). The W3C gap analysis reports that every rendering engine has trouble with pre-base vowel signs ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/2023/DNOTE-deva-gap-20230614/)), so test conjuncts on real Android and iOS devices. Never letter-space Devanagari, never clip matras in fixed-height chips, and never use faux-Devanagari Latin display fonts. Ranjana is a living revival: Nithya Ranjana won a D&AD 2024 Yellow Pencil ([D&AD](https://www.dandad.org/work/annual-2024/behind-the-work/how-calligraphy-inspired-a-new-typeface-for-an-ancient-nepalese-script)), and the script was suppressed under the Rana regime ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2024/7/6/the-long-and-joyous-road-to-revive-nepals-ancient-taboo-scripts)). Use it only as real text, such as a correctly rendered site name, and have it reviewed by practitioners like Callijatra or Nepal Lipi Guthi. No published Nepal-specific guidance from the Lumbini Development Trust or Buddhist federations on decorative use was found. Commission a review by local Buddhist and Newar advisers before launch, and do not treat these rules as complete.

## Design for a Redmi in the sun with no signal

The physical constraints are harsher than desktop design reviews assume. Nepal has **26.58M 4G users, 86% of broadband subscriptions**, but complaints about slow speeds and patchy coverage are common outside the cities. SIM penetration is above 100% because people carry several SIMs to cope with gaps ([Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/science-technology/2026/04/14/4g-keeps-growing-fast-in-nepal-while-users-still-face-slow-speeds-and-patchy-coverage)). Ookla dropped Nepal from its speed index for "inconsistent and highly variable" speeds ([NepaliTelecom](https://www.nepalitelecom.com/2025/06/nepal-out-of-ookla-mobile-broadband-speed-index.html)). Xiaomi became the number-one brand in 2025, and the budget Redmi A5 alone took about 11% of the market ([GadgetByte Nepal](https://www.gadgetbytenepal.com/xiaomi-number-1-brand-in-nepal)). The app therefore has to be offline-first. Core place data, maps, Nepali and English text, and audio are downloaded once over Wi-Fi, with an explicit "Available offline" state, queued writes for Report, and no screen gated behind a spinner. This is promise 5 put into practice. As a working target, keep the store download under about 40–50MB and offer audio and high-resolution media as optional packs labelled with their size.

Sunlight changes the contrast target. Advertised contrast is measured in darkness. Outdoor tests run at 40,000–50,000 lux, compared with roughly 100–500 lux indoors ([DisplayMate](https://displaymate.com/Mobile_Brightness_ShootOut_2.htm); [DXOMARK](https://corp.dxomark.com/catalog/services/testing-benchmarking/display-performance-evaluation/)), and some phones in simulated sunlight reach an effective contrast near 2:1 ([GSMArena](https://m.gsmarena.com/gsmarena_labs_announces_sunlit_legibility_display_test-news-4105.php)). WCAG 2.2 AA's 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for UI components ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)) is the floor. **Target ≥7:1 for primary reading text**. That number is an inference from AAA and display physics, not a published outdoor standard. Never put body text on photos without a solid scrim, and avoid thin weights. Make touch targets **48×48dp everywhere**, which meets Android's requirement ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/apps)), exceeds iOS's 44pt and easily clears WCAG's 24px ([W3C 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)). Layouts must survive 200% text and Android font scale 2.0, and Devanagari overflow will show up first. WCAG 2.5.7 means every drag needs a single-tap alternative, so the map requires a list or "nearest" view. WCAG 2.4.11 means bottom sheets must not hide focused elements. Dark mode is a genuine battery win only at high brightness: **39–47% display-power savings at 100% brightness, against 3–9% at 30–50%**, measured on OLED phones ([Purdue](https://engineering.purdue.edu/ECE/News/2021/dark-mode-may-not-save-your-phones-battery-life-as-much-as-you-think-but-there-are-a-few-silver-linings)). That is the outdoor case, but many budget phones use LCD panels. Follow the system theme, make each theme pass contrast on its own, and consider a high-contrast "outdoor" token set.

Motion and rendering should stay quiet and run on the UI thread. Reanimated defaults to `ReduceMotion.System`, which snaps animations to their end state when the user has asked for reduced motion. Its docs suggest swapping bounces for fades ([Reanimated](https://docs.swmansion.com/react-native-reanimated/docs/guides/accessibility/)). `useReducedMotion()` reads the setting only at startup. Use FlashList for long lists ([FlashList](https://shopify.github.io/flash-list/docs/)) and decode images at their display size. A full-colour bitmap costs 4 bytes per pixel ([Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2022/09/optimize-for-android-go-lessons-from-google-apps-part-1.html)), so a 4000×3000 photo in a small card wastes about 48MB. Avoid blur and stacked translucency over scrolling content, and profile release builds on a real low-end phone. On the site, pass Core Web Vitals at p75 mobile: **LCP ≤2.5s, INP ≤200ms, CLS ≤0.1** ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)). Self-host fonts with `next/font`, which generates metric-matched fallbacks to cut CLS and preloads only on the routes that use a font ([Next.js](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/components/font)). Cap the site at two families, preload Latin only, and load Devanagari only on Nepali routes. If a hero video is used, LCP is measured on the **poster**, and autoplay videos start downloading immediately even off-screen ([web.dev video](https://web.dev/patterns/web-vitals-patterns/video/video/); [web.dev performance](https://web.dev/learn/performance/video-performance?hl=en)). Keep it short, muted and without an audio track, give it a pause control (WCAG 2.2.2), and fall back to the poster under `prefers-reduced-motion`. For bilingual accessibility, set `lang` per page and per Nepali passage (WCAG 3.1.2), and add romanisation for Pali and Sanskrit. Nepali appears in TalkBack's language list, but with device caveats ([Google](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/11101402?hl=en)), and UNICEF's free "Dina" voice exists because Nepali "often get[s] pronounced incorrectly" ([ATscale/UNICEF](https://atscalepartnership.org/news-stories/unicef-and-atscale-launch-nepali-text-speech-digital-voice-dina-dainaa-enhance)). That makes pre-recorded audio guides a better bet than relying on device TTS.

## Conclusion

The central finding is that for Sākṣī, "premium" and "trustworthy" are the same design problem, and the five promises already define the solution. The visual moves that read as luxury in this category are restraint, real credited photography, quiet cartography and a confident serif face. The interaction moves that earn trust are one-tap checkable citations, "not recorded" in place of zero, "Near X?" in place of a false arrival, and "Closed: duplicate" kept separate from "Fixed". Both come down to showing the seams honestly. That inverts the usual travel-app instinct to make everything look certain and polished, and it is also what separates Sākṣī from generic AI travel products, which can produce answers but cannot prove them.

Three uncertainties should shape the next steps. First, no controlled study supports the specific depth control or the "one place, three verbs" landing narrative, so test both with real visitors at Lumbini before committing. Second, the evidence that citations inflate trust means a polished Ask feature could do harm if its retrieval is weak. Retrieval quality and the "I don't know" path deserve more engineering than the chat surface. Third, cultural guidance specific to Nepal is thin in written form, so a short review by Lumbini, Buddhist and Newar advisers is cheap insurance against the kind of public removal that ended Nepal's 2020 yeti statues.
